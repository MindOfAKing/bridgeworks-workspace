## 1. Strategy & Transformation — Direction

Primary buyers: founder, CEO, managing director, transformation or business-unit leader.  
Buying triggers: expansion, new market, multiple disconnected priorities, stalled initiative, unclear operating model.  
Diagnostic: Direction-to-Execution Brief.

### LinkedIn authority post

Most strategy problems are not idea problems.

They are translation problems.

The ambition may be clear: enter a new market, modernize operations, reposition the company, or use AI well.

Then the plan meets reality.

Ownership is split. Priorities compete. The operating model stays the same. Teams add activity without removing friction.

Before another roadmap, ask five questions:

1. What outcome must be true in 12 months?
2. Which constraint makes that outcome unlikely today?
3. Who owns the change across functions?
4. What must stop—not only start?
5. What evidence will tell us the strategy is surviving execution?

Strategy becomes valuable when it changes decisions, ownership, and operating rhythm.

Where is the gap between your ambition and the system expected to deliver it?

## 2. Digital Platforms & Brand Systems — Credibility

Primary buyers: founder, managing director, commercial lead, marketing or brand lead.  
Buying triggers: new market, premium offer, outdated site, inconsistent proof, weak enquiry path, multilingual friction.  
Diagnostic: Credibility and Conversion Review.

### LinkedIn authority post

A website does not lose trust only when it looks old.

It loses trust when the buyer has to reconcile contradictions.

One email in the header and another in the footer.  
A strong claim followed by empty proof.  
An English buyer journey interrupted by untranslated forms.  
Several services competing for one vague next step.

None of these details proves the business is weak.

But each asks the buyer to do extra work before they enquire.

A useful website review should test four things:

- Can the right buyer recognize themselves?
- Can they understand the offer without interpretation?
- Is the proof specific and consistent?
- Is the next step clear, credible, and functioning?

Good design attracts attention. A credible system removes doubt.

## 3. Content, Visibility & Demand — Demand

Primary buyers: founder, commercial, growth, brand, or marketing lead.  
Buying triggers: strong delivery but weak visibility, inconsistent publishing, category education need, new-market entry, overreliance on referrals.  
Diagnostic: Authority and Demand Gap Scan.

### LinkedIn authority post

Content is not a posting problem.

It is a commercial memory problem.

When the buyer finally needs help, can they remember:

- what you understand;
- how you think;
- what problem you solve;
- what evidence you can show;
- what next step feels safe?

Most organizations publish from a blank page. The better source is already inside the work:

decisions, diagnostics, customer questions, implementation lessons, mistakes, evidence, and tools.

The operating system is simple:

Work produces insight.  
Insight becomes buyer education.  
Buyer education becomes recognition.  
Recognition makes a later commercial conversation warmer.

Do not ask every post to generate a lead. Ask the content system to make the right company easier to trust and shortlist.

## 4. AI & Workflow Automation — Leverage

Primary buyers: founder, operations, digital, technology, innovation, or commercial lead.  
Buying triggers: repetitive handoffs, inbox/spreadsheet work, slow response, reporting burden, inconsistent follow-up, AI experiments without governance.  
Diagnostic: Workflow Friction Map.

### LinkedIn authority post

Do not automate the task first.

Map the decision first.

Many workflows look repetitive because the visible action repeats: copy data, answer an enquiry, prepare a report, chase an approval.

But the expensive part is often hidden:

- deciding what qualifies;
- finding missing context;
- resolving exceptions;
- proving that the action happened;
- knowing when a human must intervene.

A practical automation map has five layers:

1. Trigger.
2. Required context.
3. Decision rules.
4. Human approval and exception path.
5. Evidence written back to the system of record.

If those layers are unclear, automation scales ambiguity.

The first AI question should be: which decision can we make safer, faster, and more repeatable?

## 5. Execution & Operating Systems — Delivery

Primary buyers: founder, CEO, COO, operations, transformation, or program lead.  
Buying triggers: cross-functional initiative, disconnected tools, unclear ownership, weak reporting, plans that stall, delivery across markets or teams.  
Diagnostic: Execution System Health Check.

### LinkedIn authority post

Execution does not fail only because people lack discipline.

It often fails because the system cannot answer basic questions:

- What outcome are we moving?
- Who owns the next decision?
- Where is the latest evidence?
- Which dependency is blocked?
- What requires approval?
- What changed this week?

Adding another task tool rarely solves this.

The useful unit is the operating loop:

decision → owner → action → evidence → review → next decision.

CRM, tasks, files, communication, and reporting should support that loop without competing to become the truth.

When plans repeatedly stall, inspect the execution architecture before demanding more activity.
