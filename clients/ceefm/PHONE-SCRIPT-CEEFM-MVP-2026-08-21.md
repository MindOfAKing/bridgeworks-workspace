# Phone script: Victor, CEEFM home cleaning MVP

**INTERNAL. Do not send.** 21 August 2026.
Numbers: ask 2,150,000 Ft setup + 200,000 Ft/month. Floor 1,524,000 Ft setup, 150,000 Ft/month.

---

## Before you dial

Three rules for this call.

1. **State the price, then stop talking.** The silence after a number belongs to him. Whoever speaks first loses it.
2. **Never justify a price he has not challenged.** Explaining before he objects tells him the number is soft.
3. **Trade, never give.** Every time the price moves down, something leaves the scope. Say what leaves, out loud, every time.

---

## 1. Open, 30 seconds

> "I read the brief. There's a real business in it. Before we talk numbers I want to be straight with you about two things, because one of them will save you money and the other might save you a fine."

Then go to the website, then the cameras. Do not lead with price.

---

## 2. Why the current site cannot do this

He will ask, or he will assume it later and use it against your price. Get there first.

> "The site I built you is a brochure. It's fast, it ranks, it does that job well. But it's static files. There's no server behind it. That's why your contact form runs through an outside service, because the site itself can't process a form.
>
> What you're describing isn't a website. It's software. Accounts, a database, photo storage from every cleaner on every visit, recurring card payments, reports that write themselves. Static files can't do any of that, on any host, at any price.
>
> So this gets built alongside ceefm.eu, not on top of it. The two link to each other. Nothing you already paid for gets thrown away."

If he pushes: **"can't we just add it to the existing site?"**

> "Not a question of effort. It's like asking to add a second floor to a car. The site is the right tool for what it does. This is a different machine."

Concrete backup if he wants detail:

- No server: nothing runs when a visitor arrives, so no logins and no sessions
- No database: client records, visit history and schedules have nowhere to live
- No file storage: before and after photos from every visit run to gigabytes in the first year
- No background jobs: recurring billing and report generation need something running when nobody is on the site
- Access control: photos of people's homes need permissions and deletion rules that shared hosting cannot enforce

---

## 3. The cameras, before he sells them

> "One more thing before price. The brief promises in-home cameras, smart locks and AI checking the work. I'd hold all three.
>
> Cameras inside homes plus recording your own cleaners means a data protection impact assessment, and a written monitoring policy under the Munka Törvénykönyve. NAIH is exactly the regulator for this. That exposure sits on CEEFM, not on me.
>
> And you don't need it to launch. Documented visits, photo proof, tracked entry, a named cleaner. That's the trust story, it's true, and you can sell it next month instead of next year."

This is the single most valuable thing you say on this call. Say it once, plainly, then move on. Do not lecture.

---

## 4. The price

> "Setup is two million one hundred fifty thousand. Five to six weeks. Then two hundred thousand a month for care, starting a month after launch."

**Stop. Say nothing else.**

Do not add "but", "however", "I know it's a lot", or a justification. Wait for him.

If he goes quiet for a long time, the next words are a question, not a discount:

> "What's your reaction?"

---

## 5. What he will say

**"That's more than I expected."**
> "What number were you carrying?"

Make him say his number first. Then you know your gap before you concede anything.

**"The last project was four hundred euros a month."**
> "That was marketing. Monthly work, monthly fee. This is a product you'll own and sell from. Different thing, different price. If it works you'll make the setup back off eight or ten subscribers."

Do not defend the old number. Move the category.

**"You did the August work for free."**
> "I did, happily, and I'd do a favour like that again. That was an hour of my day to stop your ads burning money. This is a five week build. Different question."

Warm, once, then closed. Do not trade against it.

**"Can we start smaller?"**
> "This is already the smallest version that actually works. But there are things we can leave out. Which matters least to you, taking card payments online, or launching in all three districts on day one?"

Now he is choosing scope, not price. That is where you want him.

**"Let me think about it."**
> "Of course. The proposal holds until the twelfth of September. After that I'd have to requote, because my autumn is filling up."

Say the date out loud. He sat on the April proposal for five weeks.

**"Two hundred a month is a lot for support."**
> "It's not support, it's the thing staying alive. Payments failing, reports not sending, photos of your customers' homes sitting in storage that has to stay locked and get deleted on schedule. First thirty days are included. If you'd rather not have it, I'll build it and hand you the keys, and you find someone to run it."

Then stop. He will not want to find someone.

---

## 6. If he pushes on price

Each step removes something named. Say the trade out loud. Lead with the first one.

| Say this | Lands at |
|---|---|
| "I can drop online card payments. You invoice monthly through szamlazz, same as you do now. That takes it to one million nine hundred." | 1,914,000 |
| "We launch with one district page and add the other two once you've proven demand. A hundred and nine each, later." | 1,696,000 |
| "Hungarian only at launch, English follows." | 1,605,000 |
| "Operations view becomes a shared sheet instead of a proper screen." | 1,524,000 |

**Below 1,524,000 you say no.** Not "let me think", no.

> "I can't get there and still build something I'd put my name on. I'd rather you kept your money."

Monthly ladder: 200,000 ask, 170,000 if he fights, 150,000 floor. Below that you are under your own rate card.

---

## 7. Free things to give instead of price

Use these before you touch a number. They cost you almost nothing.

- Kickoff next week, ahead of your other work
- A third revision round instead of two
- Scoping folded into the first invoice at no extra charge
- Payment terms stretched from 8 days to 14
- A launch-day walkthrough for his cleaning staff

---

## 8. Hard no, whatever he offers

**Paying at the end instead of in three parts.**
> "Forty percent to start is how I schedule the work. That one I can't move."

He ended a sixteen week engagement at week nine. If he pushes here, that is the loudest signal on this call. Do not soften.

**Cameras folded in at the same price.** Separate build, after legal review.

**Free support instead of the monthly.** See section 5.

**Deposit below forty percent.**

---

## 9. Closing

If he is in:

> "Good. I'll send the contract today. It's the same one I use for every build: scope fixed in an annex, two revision rounds, forty thirty thirty. Sign it and I start Monday."

If he is thinking:

> "Take the week. The proposal holds to the twelfth. One thing though, that second conversion action in your Google Ads is still firing on page load. If you're spending right now, that's counting page views as leads. Worth thirty seconds in the account today."

End on something useful to him. It keeps the door open without discounting.
