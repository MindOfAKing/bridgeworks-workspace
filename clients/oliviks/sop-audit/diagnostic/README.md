# The diagnostic: how to run it

The two surveys tell you **where** to look. They do not tell you **how** any process
actually works. This folder turns survey responses into the first paid deliverable, which
is a diagnostic, not a manual.

Sequence:

```
Surveys land
     v
1. Process Inventory      what exists, who owns it, is it written
     v
2. Gap Map                intent vs reality vs evidence, per process
     v
3. Priority Register      scored, tiered, sequenced
     v
Review with Aese and Cynthia          <-- the diagnostic ends here
     v
4. Targeted follow-up     only the processes that made Tier 1
     v
5. SOP and checklist build            <-- quoted separately
```

Stopping at the review is deliberate. It is where the client sees why an SOP is needed
before paying for forty of them, and it is where you learn how many you are actually
writing.

## Filling the inventory

The template ships with a starting inventory of about fifty Oliviks processes, drawn from
the Foundation engagement and the survey areas. It is a starting point, not the answer.
Expect to delete some and add ones nobody thought to mention.

Sources, in order of weight:

1. **Observed evidence.** What you can check yourself: the Wolt listing, the shop, the
   Google profile, the site, the invoices. Highest weight because it does not depend on
   anyone's memory.
2. **Staff responses.** How the work is actually experienced.
3. **Owner responses.** How the business is intended to run.

When the three disagree, that disagreement is the finding. Do not average them.

## Two things the surveys do not measure

Both matter for scoring, and both are closed in the follow-up rather than the forms.

**Variance.** Neither form asks whether two people do the same task differently. The
owners' scale conflates "written down" with "followed", and staff are not asked to compare
themselves to colleagues. For a kitchen this is the single most diagnostic dimension.

**Frequency.** The owners' audit asks how much an area hurts, not how often it goes wrong.
"Costs us money" and "happens twice a week" are different findings that need different
responses.

Do not patch the forms to add these. The collector writes its header row once and then
appends by position, so adding fields would misalign every new row against the existing
header, and answers may already be in.

## Scoring

Four dimensions, 1 to 3 each. Total 4 to 12.

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Risk** | Annoying if wrong | Costs money or a customer | Hurts someone, breaks the law, or stops trading |
| **Frequency** | Monthly or less | Weekly | Daily or every service |
| **Variance** | Everyone does it the same | Some drift between people | Every person does it differently |
| **Dependency** | Anyone can do it | A couple of people | One person only |

| Total | Tier |
|---|---|
| 10 to 12 | **Tier 1.** Write first. Safety, money, legal, customer-facing failure. |
| 7 to 9 | **Tier 2.** Consistency. Variability is costing quality. |
| 4 to 6 | **Tier 3.** Scale. Needed as the business grows, not before. |

Then add effort as S, M or L, and sequence within each tier by highest score and lowest
effort first. A Tier 1 item that takes an hour to write beats a Tier 1 item that needs
three interviews.

Record the score components, not just the total. When Aese asks why closing sits above
catering, the answer should be four numbers rather than a judgement call.

## Sizing

Oliviks is a small kitchen. Two owners and a handful of helpers.

Aim for ten to fifteen SOPs, the checklists that come off them, and a one-page ownership
matrix. Not sixty documents, no version control register, no review calendar. A document
nobody maintains is worse than no document, because it goes stale and then gets trusted.

If the diagnostic surfaces forty candidate processes, that is fine. It means thirty of
them are Tier 3 and the honest recommendation is to leave them alone this year.

## Two questions to settle before drafting anything

**Language.** If the kitchen reads Hungarian more comfortably than English, SOPs in
English are decoration. Ask Aese directly, and ask about each person rather than in
general. This changes cost and timeline, so settle it before quoting the build.

**Existing HACCP paperwork.** HACCP is already legally required in Hungary. Some
documentation may exist. Build on it rather than duplicating it, and never produce a
parallel hygiene document that contradicts the one an inspector will ask for.

## Pulling the responses

Sheet: [Oliviks SOP audit responses](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1qQI3lp9TL4t6ykUoALdKOkVIvSCxD--QVvVQtWWdNss/edit)

The `Owners` tab carries a computed score per area and an overall. Treat those as a
conversation opener, not a finding. The free-text columns and the staff themes carry the
real signal.

Staff rows are anonymous and date-stamped only. Report them as themes across everyone.
Never quote a line only one person could have written, and never attribute a view to a
role when only one person holds that role.
