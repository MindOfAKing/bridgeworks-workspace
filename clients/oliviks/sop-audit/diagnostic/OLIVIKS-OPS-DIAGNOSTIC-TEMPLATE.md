## What this is

This is not a set of procedures. It is the map that decides which procedures are worth
writing, in what order, and why.

It comes from three sources:

1. What you and Cynthia said the business does, from the owners' audit.
2. What your team said actually happens on shift, anonymously.
3. What we checked ourselves, in your systems and listings.

Where those three agree, the process is probably fine. Where they disagree, that
disagreement is the finding. Most of this document is about the disagreements.

>
> **The gap:** There is no single list of where a dish appears, so switching one off is a

>
> **How it fails:** A customer orders a dish that is gone. The order is refunded or

>
> **What it costs:** Refunds, a bad review against a 4.8 average built over 160 reviews,

> **Needed:** Checklist, not a full SOP. One card listing every surface, in order.
>
> **Priority:** Risk 2, Frequency 3, Variance 3, Dependency 2. **Total 10, Tier 1.**

## 4. What happens next

**Stage 4, follow-up.** We do not have enough detail to write these yet. Knowing that
stock control needs standardising is not the same as knowing when it is triggered, who
does it, what counts as done, and what happens when a supplier short-delivers. That needs
[N] short conversations and one service watched in person.

Proposed:

| Who | How long | About |
|---|---|---|
| Aese | 45 min | Money, ordering, exceptions, decisions only she makes |
| Cynthia | 45 min | Kitchen flow, prep, quality standards, portioning |
| [Longest-serving team member] | 30 min | What opening, closing and orders actually look like |
| [Newest team member] | 20 min | Where the job was unclear in the first two weeks |
| Observation | one service | Order intake and handover, watched, not asked about |

**Stage 5, the build.** Each document gets drafted, checked by you, then tested by
somebody who did not write it. The test is simple and it is the only definition of
finished worth using:

> Can a competent person carry out the procedure correctly, from the document alone,
> without the person who normally does it standing next to them?

Where they hesitate, the document is incomplete and gets revised. Not before.
