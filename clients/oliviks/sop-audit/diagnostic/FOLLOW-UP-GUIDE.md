# Follow-up guide

Stage 4. Runs after the diagnostic has been reviewed with Aese and Cynthia, and only on
the processes that reached Tier 1 and the top of Tier 2.

The surveys told you where to look. These conversations tell you how the work is actually
done. The goal is not opinions, which you already have. It is the rules an experienced
person applies without noticing they are applying them.

## Ground rules

**Do not interview everyone about everything.** Match the person to the process. Three or
four conversations plus one service watched is enough for ten to fifteen documents.

**Ask for the last time, not the usual time.** "Walk me through the last delivery you
received" beats "how do you receive deliveries". People describe the intended process when
asked in general and the real one when asked about a specific instance.

**Follow the exception, not the happy path.** The happy path is easy to write and rarely
where things break. Most of the value in an SOP is the "what if" section.

**Never reveal who said what in the staff survey.** If a theme came from one person, do
not raise it in a way that identifies them. If that makes a topic un-raisable, raise it as
your own observation instead, or drop it.

## Who, and about what

| Who | Time | Cover |
|---|---|---|
| Aese | 45 min | Money, supplier relationships, exceptions, pricing decisions, catering quoting, anything only she does |
| Cynthia | 45 min | Kitchen flow, prep order, batch sizes, portioning, quality standards, what "right" tastes like |
| Longest-serving team member | 30 min | Opening, closing, order handling as actually performed |
| Newest team member | 20 min | Where the job was unclear in week one, what they had to work out alone |

If the team is only Aese and Cynthia plus casual help, drop to two interviews and lean
harder on observation.

## Closing the two survey gaps

Neither form measures these, and both feed the priority score. Ask them early.

### Variance

Ask about a process, then:

- If Cynthia does this and someone else does it, what comes out differently?
- Show me how much of this goes in a container. Now, with the actual scoop.
- Has anyone ever done this in a way that surprised you?
- If I watched three different people do this, what would I see change?

For portioning, do not accept a verbal answer. Ask them to serve one and weigh or
photograph it. Portion variance is invisible in conversation and obvious on a scale.

### Frequency

- How many times a week does this go wrong?
- When was the last time? And before that?
- Out of ten of these, how many come out exactly right?

A number, even a rough one, changes the priority score. "Sometimes" does not.

## Extracting a process

Per Tier 1 process, fill this. It becomes the SOP directly.

```
PROCESS
[Name]

TRIGGER
What starts it. A time, an event, a signal, a person asking.

OWNER
Who does it. Who covers when they are not there.

INPUTS
People, tools, equipment, ingredients, information, access.

STEPS
1.
2.
3.
Order matters. Note where it does not.

DECISION POINTS
Where the person has to choose. What they choose between.
What tells them which way to go.

QUALITY CRITERIA
How they know each step is done right, not just done.

EXCEPTIONS
What if the ingredient is out.
What if the delivery is short.
What if the customer changes the order.
What if the rider is early.
What if the equipment fails.
Aim for at least three real ones per process.

ESCALATION
When they stop and ask someone. Who. How.

RECORD
What gets written down, where, by whom.

DONE
One sentence. What correct execution looks like from the outside.
```

The exceptions section is where most of the value sits. If you get through a process and
have no exceptions written down, you have not finished the conversation.

## Observation

One service, watched. Preferably a Friday or Saturday lunch, which the owners' audit
should confirm as the busiest window.

Watch, do not ask. Questions during service change behaviour and annoy people. Write
questions down and ask afterwards.

What to watch for:

- Where does an order enter, and does anything catch every channel in one place
- How many times does someone ask someone else a question they should not need to ask
- What gets checked before an order leaves, and by whom
- What happens at courier handover, and how the right bag finds the right rider
- Where people wait, hunt for something, or do something twice
- What Aese or Cynthia get pulled into that nobody else can resolve
- Portion sizes, if more than one person is plating

That last one is the whole reason to be there in person.

## After

Update the Gap Map with what you learned. Scores will move, and some Tier 1 items will
drop once you find a process is already consistent and simply unwritten. That is a good
outcome and worth telling the client, because it shows the priority order was earned
rather than assumed.

Then, and only then, start drafting.
